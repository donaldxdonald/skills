# Branding Discovery Checklist

Use this when preparing a client workshop, brand sprint, rebrand audit, or identity brief.

## Business Context

- What business goal does the brand work need to support?
- What is changing now: new market, launch, pricing, audience, competition, trust problem, growth plateau, acquisition, or internal confusion?
- What happens if nothing changes?
- Who approves strategy, identity, copy, and rollout?

## Audience

- Who is the primary audience?
- What alternatives do they compare?
- What language do they use to describe the problem?
- What do they need to believe before buying, joining, sharing, or trusting?
- Which audience is intentionally not the priority?

## Positioning

- What category should the brand be placed in?
- What is the sharpest point of difference?
- What promise can the brand credibly make?
- What proof supports that promise?
- What should customers remember after one interaction?

## Expression

- What should the brand feel like: calm, bold, technical, premium, playful, clinical, rebellious, warm, authoritative?
- What should it never feel like?
- Which brands are useful references, and exactly why?
- Which visual or verbal cliches should be avoided in this category?
- What accessibility, localization, production, or platform constraints apply?

## Touchpoints

- Website
- Product UI or service experience
- Sales deck and pitch
- Email and onboarding
- Social and community replies
- PR and founder narrative
- Packaging, signage, print, invoices, or physical spaces
- Internal docs and recruiting

## Deliverables

- Strategy brief
- Messaging framework
- Naming or tagline options
- Visual territories
- Identity system
- Brand guidelines
- Asset library
- Launch and migration plan

## Red Flags

- The client asks for "just a logo" but describes trust, differentiation, or conversion problems.
- Sales, website, support, and PR tell different stories.
- The audience is "everyone."
- Competitors are listed but not analyzed.
- Moodboards are aesthetic only, with no intended perception.
- The team cannot explain what must be preserved from the old brand.
